# Buyer-Quality Rubric

Use this when separating real buyer signal from cheap lead volume.

## Score Dimensions

| Dimension | Strong Signal | Weak Signal |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| ICP fit | target role, company size, industry, geography | student, vendor, tiny company, irrelevant role |
| Pain fit | names the expensive problem the offer solves | generic curiosity or "just researching" |
| Intent | requested help, demo, audit, pricing, follow-up | downloaded something with no urgency |
| Sales acceptance | accepted, worked, opportunity, customer | rejected, no-show, invalid, spam |
| Offer match | lead wants the promised next step | lead expected something else |
| Handoff quality | sales sees ad/form/post context | sales receives a cold record with no story |

## Diagnosis Pattern

For each campaign or source, classify:

- **Real signal:** qualified buyer evidence exists
- **Fake signal:** platform metrics look good but downstream quality is weak
- **Unknown:** data is insufficient
- **Leak:** good interest is being lost in form, offer, tracking, or handoff

## Root Cause Map

| Symptom | Likely Cause |
|---------|--------------|
| high spend, no leads | offer, audience, tracking, or form path |
| many leads, low quality | audience too broad, form too easy, offer too generic |
| good comments, few leads | conversion path or offer mismatch |
| good leads, sales confused | handoff lacks campaign/pain/form context |
| strong CPL, weak CRM | platform optimization is chasing the wrong proxy |

## What Not To Do

- Do not scale because CPL is low.
- Do not pause a campaign with strong downstream quality just because CPL is above target.
- Do not add form friction before identifying the bad-fit pattern.
- Do not rewrite creative when tracking or CRM mapping is the real issue.

## Output

Every buyer-quality diagnosis should include:

- evidence
- likely root cause
- why this matters
- what to do next
- what not to optimize yet
